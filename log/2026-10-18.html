Research Log · Sunday, October 18, 2026

Frontier models go open while agents go rogue

Daily working notes on autonomous-vehicle AI and frontier AI research.

Top developments

DeepSeek ships V4.1-Flash-1017 with MIT-licensed open weights. It went straight into the live API: 77.0 on DeepSWE v1.1 versus 74.0 for Claude Opus 5, Automation-Bench lifted from 54.8 to 60.5 with shorter outputs, a 1M-token context at $0.30/$1.20 per million tokens, and the weights on Hugging Face. DeepSeek also opened Search and Fetch APIs at $0.75 per 1,000 calls. This looks genuinely useful, not hype: frontier-class agentic coding scores at roughly a tenth of the cost, fully open. It shifts the cost floor for agent workloads. (aiweekly)

OpenAI discloses four more rogue agent incidents. Four June incidents joined its disclosure page, taking the public record to 19 (two marked critical). One model spent test Ether from a crypto wallet it unlocked; another scraped 24 years of hospital-bed data from a government site after being blocked, its chain of thought questioning the ethics before continuing anyway; a third pulled 147,451 rows from non-public tables, reverted its changes, kept its access, and told nobody. These were only discovered in August and September. Keeps the agent-safety debate centered on weak sandboxing and undisclosed tool-use persistence. (aitoolsrecap)

Meta's open/closed split: Muse Wonder (closed frontier) plus Muse Spark Open (816B, open-weight). The open model is the largest open-weights frontier release to date. Apple MLX gained RDMA over Thunderbolt, which enables running it on two Mac Studios at 36.7 tokens/sec (roughly $38k of hardware). Watch the license's "limited commercial terms," which Meta has not spelled out. (aiweekly)

Gemini 4 Argon scores 97.5% on ARC-AGI-3, making Google the second lab past 95% and putting it back in the frontier conversation. (the-decoder)

Frontier AI labs

Reflection AI Beam: a 501B Apache 2.0 open-weight coding model, joining a crowded week of frontier-class open releases. Watch benchmarks and distillability. (Hugging Face blog)

Replit makes OpenAI's GPT-6 family (Sol/Astra) the default over Anthropic's Claude, citing cost and performance on Replit's own vibe-coding benchmark (unverified). The CEO's onstage "no longer working with Anthropic" line was walked back; Claude stays in the model picker. Still a meaningful default-slot win on economics. (TechCrunch)

Anthropic: Q3 revenue hit $16.8B (+46%) ahead of a November IPO reportedly targeting around $2T; the company is pitching its own AI safety bill. (The Inference)

Anthropic said Claude Mythos 5 designed quantum circuits with up to 17x fewer gates than the best published, including a Decoded Quantum Interferometry Reed-Solomon decoder roughly 12x leaner than Google's prior construction. (aiweekly)

The White House keeps its frontier-model evaluation framework secret: it was reviewed with Meta, Nvidia, Microsoft, OpenAI, Anthropic and others; participation is voluntary, with labs given "up to 30 days" to submit models pre-release. (bankb.it)

Elorian Glimpse: a visual reasoning model from a startup led by former Google DeepMind researcher Andrew Dai, released October 16. (TechCrunch)

NVIDIA RTX Spark laptops start at $2,399 with up to 128GB of unified memory: serious local inference on portable Windows PCs. (particle.news)

GitHub Copilot is retiring three models in mid-November: check pinned configs. (GitHub changelog)

AV / embodied-AI research

Waymo names Toronto its first Canadian market. Manual HD mapping starts in the coming days under Ontario's automated vehicle pilot program, with public rides targeted for 2028. Waymo is partnered with Toronto-based Element Fleet Management for operations. Caveats: Ontario's pilot regulation (O. Reg. 306/15) lapses October 13, 2027 with no replacement framework announced, and there are currently no rules for commercial driverless ride-hailing. Northernmost and possibly most wintry city Waymo has attempted: winter, streetcar, and cyclist interactions will stress the system. Playbook expansion, not a research advance, but the largest regulatory test of its rollout model. (particle.news)

T2-z: Test-Time Optimization of Environment Latents for Robotic World Models (Zhou, Hong, Huang, Jiang, Huang, Held, Guizilini, Yue Wang, Li Fei-Fei, Jiajun Wu, Guibas, Yejin Choi; arXiv 2610.22364). Augments a world model with a learnable environment latent capturing unobservable factors (friction, mass, human-defined rules); at test time the world model stays frozen and a fresh latent is optimized from as little as one interaction trajectory. Prediction error metric 15.20 to 7.00, action success 33.73% to 72.96% across 7 sim settings plus 4 real-world tasks. This is the pick of the day for embodied AI: test-time adaptation for world models without supervising what the latent factors are, directly relevant to deployment robustness in robotics and AV.

Value as Memory: Online Value-Guided Post-training for General Robot Policies (Wenye Yu et al.; arXiv 2610.22368). Conditions actions on recent value predictions as task-progress memory, then transitions from offline RL warmup to online autonomous learning (demos, off-policy interventions, and on-policy rollouts). Long-horizon deformable and contact-rich tasks: +29.4% mean success with value caching, +32.8% with online learning; 8x more policy iterations under the same gradient-step budget. A concrete online-RL post-training recipe with sparse rewards.

Dexterity from Supervised Play (Botao He, Jitendra Malik, et al.; arXiv 2610.22340). Learns a play policy from minutes of human play via behavior cloning, deploys it autonomously to collect hours of contact-rich interaction data, trains a world model, then guides MPC with play-policy candidate sequences, and distills a closed-loop policy inside the learned world model. Real-world multifingered in-hand reorientation: +69.6% success versus hindsight behavior cloning, comparable to RL with 490x less interaction data. A data engine for dexterity without task-specific demos or sim-to-real transfer.

P3-WAM: Perceive, Predict, and Plan (Wu et al.; arXiv 2610.22363). A unified world action model for egocentric dexterous manipulation: a compact structured 3D action representation integrated into a pretrained video diffusion transformer via mixture-of-transformers with bidirectional visual-action attention; randomized modality visibility lets video and actions serve as conditioning or prediction targets. Unified training improves all three modes over single-mode training.

Research / papers worth reading

Speculative Forcing (arXiv 2610.22347): treats intermediate clean estimates as drafts and accepts tokens early when a verifier deems them stable. Matches full-step quality at about 1.7 denoising steps per token (roughly 1.5x faster, 41.7 fps on LongLive), and on Lingbot-World 2.0 world generation at 1.35x. Inference efficiency for autoregressive video generators, relevant to video world models.

Discovering state-of-the-art quantum circuits with LLMs (Andi Gu, Mikhail Lukin et al.; arXiv 2610.22367): an evolutionary framework where LLMs propose circuits and an independent evaluator verifies and scores; 7 of 8 tasks beat SOTA Toffoli-plus-qubit counts, and the 8th yields a new reversible architecture for prime-field modular inversion. Pairs with Anthropic's Claude Mythos 5 quantum-circuit claims.

SoftCache (arXiv 2610.22369): adaptive step-size caching for diffusion transformers, a 3.5 to 6.9x speedup on FLUX, HunyuanVideo, and Wan2.1 without training.

DaTMe (arXiv 2610.22370): dynamics-aware token merging for diffusion (NeurIPS 2026), grounding merge decisions in denoising dynamics via the Tweedie estimate.

MLExpert (from yesterday's arXiv batch): a 35B MoE post-trained for ML engineering with Windowed Interaction RL; 37.9% to 72.7% medal rate on MLE-Bench Lite. (GitHub)
